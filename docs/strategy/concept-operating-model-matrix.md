# Tskaltubo Concept & Operating Model Matrix

## Purpose

This document defines the **concept space currently being evaluated for Tskaltubo**.

It is not a final business model. Its purpose is to make clear, in one place:

- which senior-living, care, rehabilitation and wellness models are under consideration;
- which services are distinct building blocks;
- which combinations of those building blocks form plausible Tskaltubo concepts;
- what is core, optional, partner-delivered, deferred or excluded in each concept;
- what evidence is still required before selecting a target operating model.

The terminology below is based on the senior-living and care continuum already developed for the project. The key structural distinction is between the **residential/care continuum** and the **parallel wellness, rehabilitation and post-acute layer**.

---

## 1. Concept model

Tskaltubo should be analysed across four independent dimensions.

### A. Residential and care intensity

`Independent Living → Supported / Assisted Living → Personal Care → Nursing Care → Skilled Nursing`

Dependency, staffing requirements, regulation, liability and operating cost generally increase from left to right.

### B. Clinical and recovery services

`Wellness / Prevention → Rehabilitation → Transitional / Post-Acute Care`

This is a separate axis. Rehabilitation and post-acute care are not simply "more intensive senior living"; they can serve both permanent residents and temporary patients.

### C. Length of stay

- Long-term residence
- Medium-term recovery or rehabilitation stay
- Short-term respite or wellness stay
- Day / outpatient services

### D. Delivery model

- In-house
- Partner-delivered
- Optional add-on
- Later phase
- Not part of the concept

This separation matters because the strategic question is not simply **"retirement home or rehabilitation clinic?"** Tskaltubo can combine selected residential, support, care, rehabilitation and wellness modules in different ways.

## 4. What each scenario actually means

### TSK-S1 — Independent Retirement Residence

**Primary proposition:** attractive long-term retirement living for largely independent residents.

The commercial engine is housing, hospitality, dining, community, housekeeping, activities and access to wellness infrastructure. Care is deliberately light and can initially be brought in through partners.

**Central question:** Is there enough DACH demand for a destination retirement proposition without making care the primary product?

---

### TSK-S2 — Assisted Living Community

**Primary proposition:** retirement living with a reliable layer of everyday assistance.

This adds personal support and activities-of-daily-living assistance while still avoiding the operational complexity of a full nursing institution.

**Central question:** Does adding assisted living materially enlarge the addressable market and resident lifetime without making the model too staff-intensive?

---

### TSK-S3 — Life Plan / Continuing Care Retirement Community

**Primary proposition:** residents can remain inside one ecosystem as their needs change.

Independent living, assisted living, nursing and rehabilitation are combined on one campus or within one coordinated operating system.

**Central question:** Is sufficient scale available to support several care intensities efficiently, and can the regulatory and staffing burden be managed in Georgia for an international resident base?

---

### TSK-S4 — Rehabilitation & Post-Acute Campus

**Primary proposition:** Tskaltubo as a destination for structured recovery, rehabilitation and post-hospital care.

Permanent senior living becomes secondary. Occupancy depends more heavily on patient flows, medical referral channels and short/medium-term stays.

**Central question:** Can Tskaltubo build defensible international referral demand and the clinical credibility required for cross-border rehabilitation?

---

### TSK-S5 — Wellness & Preventive Senior Living

**Primary proposition:** a lower-medical-intensity retirement and wellness destination built around prevention, healthy ageing, physiotherapy, balneology and quality of life.

**Central question:** Is the wellness proposition strong enough to command durable residential demand rather than functioning mainly as tourism?

---

### TSK-S6 — Integrated Tskaltubo Hybrid

**Primary proposition:** a long-term senior-living community with assisted-living capability, combined with a strong rehabilitation, physiotherapy, wellness and balneology platform that can also serve short-stay and external clients.

Higher-intensity nursing and post-acute services can initially be partner-delivered and added in-house only when demand, regulation and scale justify them.

This scenario deliberately combines two economic engines:

1. **Recurring residential revenue** from long-term residents.
2. **Service / recovery revenue** from rehabilitation, wellness, short stays and external users.

**Central question:** Does this combination create enough differentiation and revenue diversification to justify the additional operational complexity?

---

### TSK-S7 — Nursing-Led Care Facility

**Primary proposition:** high-dependency long-term care.

This is included as a comparator because it is one possible interpretation of a retirement/care project, but it should be evaluated separately from the more hospitality-, community- and rehabilitation-led concepts.

**Central question:** Does the lower-cost Georgian operating environment create enough economic advantage to compensate for the staffing, licensing, liability and reputational burden of a nursing-led model?

## 6. Evaluation framework

Every scenario should be evaluated against the same criteria.

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Demand | Is there a credible customer segment with willingness and ability to pay? |
| DACH fit | Is the proposition attractive to German, Swiss and Austrian customers? |
| Tskaltubo advantage | Does the model exploit something genuinely distinctive about Tskaltubo? |
| Property fit | Can existing sanatorium assets physically support the model? |
| Capex intensity | How much rehabilitation, medical fit-out and specialist infrastructure is required? |
| Staffing intensity | How many staff and which licensed professions are required per resident/patient? |
| Regulatory complexity | What Georgian licences, healthcare rules, facility standards and cross-border issues arise? |
| Medical liability | How much clinical responsibility does the operator assume? |
| Revenue stability | How recurring and predictable is revenue? |
| Revenue diversity | Can the model earn from several compatible customer groups? |
| Occupancy risk | How damaging are vacancy and seasonal demand? |
| Operating margin | What margin can realistically remain after staffing, property and clinical costs? |
| Scale threshold | At what occupancy / bed count does the model become economically viable? |
| Phaseability | Can the concept start simple and add complexity only after demand is proven? |
| Partnership potential | Which services can credibly be outsourced or co-operated? |
| Defensibility | Is there a durable advantage beyond lower Georgian costs? |
| Reputational risk | What failures would materially damage trust with residents, families or referral partners? |
| Exit / optionality | Can the asset be repurposed if the first concept underperforms? |

## 7. Evidence status

Use the following scenario decision statuses consistently throughout the repository. These describe concept selection, not the verification status of individual claims; claim-level evidence follows the [research standard](../research-standard.md).

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Hypothesis** | Plausible idea, not yet adequately evidenced |
| **Researching** | Active evidence collection underway |
| **Supported** | Material evidence supports the proposition |
| **Shortlisted** | Candidate for deeper feasibility modelling |
| **Preferred** | Current leading concept |
| **Rejected** | Evidence indicates the concept should not be pursued |
| **Deferred** | Potentially attractive later, but not appropriate for the current phase |

No scenario should become **Preferred** merely because it sounds strategically attractive. The status should change only when evidence exists on demand, regulation, staffing, property fit and unit economics.

## 9. Repository usage rule

This file should be the **single source of truth for the concept space**.

When research is added elsewhere in the repository:

- reference the scenario ID (`TSK-S1` … `TSK-S7`);
- reference the relevant building-block ID (`R1`, `C1`, `W1`, etc.);
- distinguish factual evidence from assumptions;
- attach sources and retrieval dates to factual claims;
- do not silently redefine a scenario in another document;
- update this matrix when the definition or status of a scenario changes.

Example:

```text
Finding:
German residents aged 75+ show X demand characteristic.

Relevant scenarios:
TSK-S1, TSK-S2, TSK-S3, TSK-S5, TSK-S6

Relevant building blocks:
R1, R4

Evidence / assumption:
Hypothetical example; replace with sourced evidence.

Implication:
...

Source:
Destatis GENESIS ...

Retrieval date:
YYYY-MM-DD
```

This keeps market research, property analysis, regulatory work and financial modelling tied to the same strategic vocabulary.

## 10. Decision sequence

The project should progressively answer:

1. **Who is the primary customer?**
2. **What length of stay are we selling?**
3. **What residential/care intensity is required?**
4. **Which Tskaltubo-specific wellness/rehabilitation services create differentiation?**
5. **Which clinical services must be in-house and which can be partner-delivered?**
6. **Which property types can physically support the shortlisted mix?**
7. **What is the resulting staffing, licensing and capex burden?**
8. **Does the unit economics support the concept?**
9. **Which scenario preserves the most useful expansion and fallback options?**

Only after these questions are evidenced should the repository collapse from a broad concept matrix into a single target operating model.
