## 1. Executive decision summary

### 1.1 Overall verdict

The July 2026 pitch deck is materially stronger than the July 2024 concept brief and the September 2025 one-pagers. It has become a plausible **pre-feasibility and confidential market-sounding document**. Its central insight is sound: historic acquisition prices are not the main economic question. Technical condition, conversion scope, staffing, occupancy, clinical governance, payer reality and phasing determine whether the project works.

The current materials nevertheless over-combine several distinct care and hospitality businesses, assume margins rather than building them from a roster and departmental budget, and imply a level of clinical, regulatory, property and demand certainty that has not yet been established.

The correct decision is:

> **Advance the concept to a structured validation sprint, but do not commit to a building, partner, full-scale development or external return claim yet.**

### 1.2 Readiness by action

| Proposed action | Current readiness | Conditions before proceeding |
|---|---|---|
| Confidential exploratory call with an experienced DACH operator | **Yes, after corrections** | Use the new name; define the discussion as discovery; remove unsupported claims, internal tactics and implied government endorsement. |
| Request for non-binding operator feedback | **Yes** | Provide a clear Phase 1 product hypothesis and an explicit list of unresolved questions. |
| Conditional expression of interest from an operator | **Not yet** | Product, liability perimeter, Georgian licensing route, operator role, pilot scope and conditions precedent must be defined. |
| Technical partnership discussion on mineral-water hydrotherapy | **Yes, exploratory only** | Ask for technical scope, water analysis requirements, protocols, training and licensing input. Do not request a broad endorsement of the whole project. |
| Government or municipality presentation | **Not yet** | Establish sponsor structure, service definition, current asset status, public benefit, resident safeguards and a credible financing/delivery path. |
| Binding or substantive property LOI | **No** | Current title, cadastral, encumbrance, occupant, heritage, utility, water-right and technical packages are required first. |
| Investor solicitation | **No** | Independent quantity-surveyor cost plan, bottom-up operations model, ramp-up, tax, financing, FX, downside case and sponsor equity plan are missing. |
| Full 100–140-bed launch | **No** | Demand, staffing, clinical network and phaseability are unproven. |
| 30–50-bed pilot | **Potentially** | Only if it is legally, physically and economically separable from the rest of the building and has a standalone capex, opex and commissioning plan. |

### 1.3 What is currently defensible

- Tskaltubo has a real historic spa identity, existing sanatorium stock and mineral-water infrastructure.
- The adaptive-reuse opportunity is worth screening.
- The DACH long-term-care market faces demographic pressure and constrained capacity.
- A private-pay, German-speaking care proposition in Georgia is a differentiable hypothesis.
- A phased approach is safer than immediate full redevelopment.
- A DACH partner may add value through brand, quality management, training, audits and referral access.
- A local Georgian structure is necessary for property, licensing, employment, construction and public-authority interfaces.
- Current project economics are highly sensitive to capex, price, occupancy and mix.

### 1.4 What is not currently defensible

- That DACH demographic demand proves willingness to relocate frail older adults to Georgia.
- That German, Austrian or Swiss public care and health benefits will fund a Georgian placement.
- That “DACH standards” are one defined or contractually transferable standard.
- That a DACH brand and audit role automatically makes that party the licensed or legally responsible operator.
- That mineral water provides the clinical outcomes implied by “healing water,” “cure” or condition-specific benefit claims.
- That Georgian wage averages prove the cost or availability of German-speaking nurses and therapists.
- That historic list values are current purchase offers or that state ownership means willingness to transact.
- That large historic floorplates reduce capex per bed. Inefficient net-to-gross ratios can increase capex.
- That a PropCo/OpCo structure improves total-project returns. It reallocates risk and return; it does not create economics by itself.
- That a 30–50-bed pilot is automatically low-capex. Central infrastructure may still need near-full investment.

### 1.5 Five issues that decide the project

1. **Product definition:** Which residents and patients will actually be admitted, and which services are in Phase 1?
2. **Payer and demand proof:** Will families pay the net private cost after any loss of home-country benefits, and in sufficient numbers?
3. **Asset conversion cost:** Can a legally available, unoccupied and phaseable building be converted within a viable cost ceiling?
4. **Licensed operating model:** Which Georgian entity is legally accountable for care, rehabilitation, medication, clinical decisions and emergencies?
5. **Workforce and emergency network:** Can the project safely staff 24/7 care with reliable German-language coverage and hospital escalation?

## 2. Name, category and positioning

### 2.1 Canonical naming architecture

| Use | Approved wording |
|---|---|
| Canonical project name | **Tskaltubo Senior Living & Care Development** |
| Short name | **Tskaltubo** |
| German cover title | **Tskaltubo: Entwicklung eines deutschsprachigen Angebots für Seniorenwohnen, Langzeitpflege und Rehabilitation** |
| German cover subtitle | **Vorprüfung und Betreiberpartnerschaft \| Tskaltubo, Georgien** |
| Formal English descriptor | **Adaptive-Reuse Feasibility for a German-Speaking Senior Living, Long-Term Care and Rehabilitation Residence in Tskaltubo, Georgia** |
| Project-stage descriptor | **Indicative pre-feasibility and partner-development initiative** |

“DACH” belongs in the market and operator description, not in the formal project name. Germany, Austria and Switzerland have different care systems, benefits, contracting rules, professional frameworks and customer economics.

### 2.2 Category definition

The project should not be described as a “premier retirement home,” a “care and cure” destination, or a single blended “senior-living and rehab” product. The precise category is:

> **A phased adaptive-reuse development for German-speaking residential long-term care, with separately validated post-acute rehabilitation and mineral-water-based hydrotherapy modules, in Tskaltubo, Georgia.**

Independent senior living, assisted living and dementia care may be future modules, but they should not be represented as part of the financially validated base case until each is separately designed and modelled.

### 2.3 Retired and replacement language

| Retire | Use instead | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Care & Cure | Senior Living, Long-Term Care and Rehabilitation | “Cure” implies a clinical outcome and creates legal and reputational risk. |
| Pflege & Kur | Seniorenwohnen, Langzeitpflege und Rehabilitation | Separates residential care from medical rehabilitation. |
| Heilwasser / healing waters | mineral-water-based hydrotherapy | Describes the intervention without promising efficacy. |
| DACH standards / DACH-grade care | operator-defined QMS benchmarked against named standards and compliant with Georgian law | DACH is not one regulatory standard. |
| Pflegeheim im Ausland | German-speaking residential long-term-care development in Georgia | More precise and less promotional. |
| skilled nursing | residential long-term care or defined 24/7 nursing care | “Skilled nursing” is mainly US terminology. |
| premium retirement home | senior living and care development | Avoids unsupported quality positioning. |
| gentle daily routines | structured daily living and activation programme | Operationally clearer. |
| medical adjacency | defined clinical network and emergency-transfer arrangements | “Adjacency” is vague. |
| trial stays | short-stay assessment or respite programme | Clarifies the purpose and admissions criteria. |

### 2.4 Approved core description

> **Tskaltubo Senior Living & Care Development is an indicative pre-feasibility and partner-development initiative for the phased adaptive reuse of a historic sanatorium in Tskaltubo, Georgia. The initial concept combines German-speaking residential long-term care with separately validated rehabilitation and mineral-water hydrotherapy modules. The base case is private-pay. The site, service mix, clinical scope, operating structure, licensing pathway and financing remain subject to diligence.**

### 2.5 Positioning principle

The project should be positioned as an **evidence-led development opportunity**, not as a finished healthcare product. The immediate ask is for expert validation and partner fit, not endorsement of a completed business case.

## 4. Project definition

### 4.1 Development hypothesis

The project proposes the adaptive reuse of one former sanatorium in Tskaltubo into a German-speaking residence combining long-term care and a distinct short-stay rehabilitation offer. Mineral-water hydrotherapy may become an adjunctive module if water rights, technical supply, safety, licensing and clinical protocols are established.

### 4.2 Working scale hypothesis

| Stage | Indicative capacity | Purpose | Non-negotiable gate |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Phase 0 | No operations | Product, legal, demand, asset and cost validation | No site commitment until title and red-flag technical review. |
| Phase 1 | 30–50 beds | Test admissions, price, referrals, staffing, German coverage and emergency systems | Physically and economically separable pilot. |
| Phase 2 | 80–100 beds | Establish scale and stable referral channels | Demonstrated quality, occupancy and unit economics. |
| Phase 3 | 100–140 beds | Full site or flagship operation | Expansion only after explicit board/investor gate. |

The target capacity is not a commitment. It must follow the selected asset, building efficiency, clinical programme, staffing model and proven demand.

### 4.3 Target customer groups

Potential segments must be validated separately:

1. German-speaking private-pay older adults requiring residential long-term care.
2. Families seeking a lower net private cost than comparable domestic alternatives.
3. Medically stable adults requiring elective 7–21-day post-acute rehabilitation.
4. Georgian or regional residents who value German-language or DACH-linked care.
5. Georgian returnees from DACH countries, as residents, employees or referral connectors.
6. Future assisted-living or independent-living residents, only if separately designed and priced.

### 4.4 Base-case payer

The only prudent committed payer case at this stage is **private payment**. Public benefits, insurer reimbursement, hospital contracts, rehabilitation referrals and capacity agreements are possible upside only after written country- and payer-specific confirmation.

### 4.5 Project success definition

The project succeeds only if all five dimensions work together:

- safe and licensable care;
- a resident proposition with proven willingness to relocate and pay;
- an asset that can be legally controlled and affordably converted;
- a workforce and clinical network that operate reliably in Georgia;
- investor-level returns after full capex, ramp-up, tax, financing and replacement costs.

## 5. Product and clinical perimeter

### 5.1 The current concept combines six different businesses

| Service line | Customer need | Operational intensity | Likely licensing/clinical burden | Included in current financial model? |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Independent senior living | Housing, community and hospitality | Low to moderate | Primarily residential and hospitality, subject to exact services | **No** |
| Assisted living / Betreuung | Support with daily living | Moderate | Depends on personal-care and medication scope | **No** |
| Residential long-term nursing care | 24/7 care for dependency and chronic conditions | High | High clinical, staffing, medication and safeguarding burden | **Yes, represented by 98 beds** |
| Dementia care | Secure, specialist cognitive and behavioural support | High | Specialist design, staffing, safeguarding and admissions criteria | **No separate model** |
| Post-acute rehabilitation | Time-limited clinical recovery programme | High | Clinical governance, medical oversight, referral and discharge protocols | **Yes, represented by 42 beds** |
| Wellness and mineral-water hydrotherapy | Adjunctive wellbeing or prescribed therapy | Moderate to high | Water safety, technical systems, clinical claims and applicable licensing | **Revenue not separately modelled** |

The deck presents all six as if they form one modular offer. They do not share the same admissions pathway, staffing, liability, building programme, length of stay, payer or sales channel.

### 5.2 Required Phase 1 service decision

The current working hypothesis is **approximately 70% long-term care and 30% rehabilitation/short stay**, because that matches the 98/42-bed full-scale model. It must be treated as a hypothesis, not as the final Phase 1 design.

Before property selection, prepare a service-definition matrix for each proposed module with:

- resident or patient profile;
- admission and exclusion criteria;
- dependency or acuity range;
- nursing and medical coverage;
- therapy intensity;
- medication and controlled-drug responsibility;
- required room, bathroom and common-space design;
- emergency escalation criteria;
- length of stay and discharge route;
- price and payer;
- legal classification and licence;
- named accountable entity and clinical leader.

### 5.3 Recommended initial boundaries

Subject to operator and Georgian clinical/legal review:

- Treat long-term care as the primary recurring-revenue service.
- Treat rehabilitation as a separately governed clinical programme with its own referral, admission and discharge criteria.
- Defer dedicated dementia care until secure design, specialist staffing and behavioural-emergency capability are proven.
- Exclude independent and assisted-living apartments from the base financial case unless their unit count, service package and price are modelled separately.
- Offer hydrotherapy only as an adjunct under defined water analysis, safety, medical-review and protocol conditions.
- Do not admit unstable acute cases or needs beyond the site's demonstrated capability.

### 5.4 Admissions and exclusions framework

The licensed operator and medical lead must approve the final criteria. A Phase 1 framework should at minimum distinguish:

| Candidate for assessment | Exclude until capability is proven |
|---|---|
| Medically stable long-term-care residents within the approved dependency range | Unstable acute illness or need for hospital-level monitoring |
| Post-operative or post-illness rehabilitation cases with a documented referral and care plan | Ventilation, complex intensive nursing or treatments unavailable locally |
| Residents whose medication and mobility needs can be safely supported | Unmanaged behavioural risk, elopement risk or severe dementia without a dedicated unit |
| Residents with a verified payment, consent and family-contact arrangement | Cases without legal consent, guardianship or financial authority documentation |
| Cases with a clear emergency-transfer and repatriation plan | Needs exceeding local hospital, imaging, laboratory, dialysis or pharmacy access |

### 5.5 Quality promise

The quality promise must be measurable. Replace broad “DACH quality” language with a schedule of named policies, audit measures and outcomes, such as:

- medication reconciliation and error rates;
- falls, pressure injuries and infection indicators;
- response times and emergency transfers;
- care-plan completion and review;
- staff competence, language coverage and turnover;
- complaints and safeguarding cases;
- rehabilitation goal attainment and unplanned readmission;
- resident and family communication standards;
- hydrotherapy adverse-event and water-safety records.

## 6. Market, demand and payer assessment

### 6.1 Macro demand is real but insufficient

Germany had almost **5.7 million people classified as needing long-term care at the end of 2023**, according to the German Federal Statistical Office. Destatis also projects that demographic ageing alone could increase the number of people needing care by **37% by 2055**. These facts support the broad need for capacity, but they do not establish demand for relocation to Georgia. See [Destatis, 2023 care recipients](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2024/12/PD24_478_224.html) and [Destatis long-term-care projection](https://www.destatis.de/EN/Themes/Society-Environment/Health/Long-Term-Care/_node.html).

The project needs evidence for a much narrower proposition:

> A defined number of German-speaking families will accept geographic distance, a different legal and healthcare system, private payment and travel/repatriation risk in exchange for price, language, quality, environment and family communication.

### 6.2 Germany, Austria and Switzerland are not one market

| Dimension | Germany | Austria | Switzerland |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public care benefit | Governed by SGB XI; foreign-residence rules are critical | Pflegegeld and residence/export conditions require case-specific analysis | Mandatory insurance and foreign-treatment rules require case-specific analysis |
| Health benefit | Statutory health benefits generally have foreign-stay restrictions, subject to exceptions | National and EU/coordination rules differ | Coverage depends on residence, insurance status and treatment circumstances |
| Customer economics | Compare project price with lost benefits, domestic co-pay and family travel | Run a separate net-cost bridge | Run a separate net-cost bridge in CHF and EUR |
| Contracting and consumer expectations | German-language contract and complaint model | Austrian legal and benefit review | Swiss contract, insurance and currency review |
| Demand validation | Separate sample and channels | Separate sample and channels | Separate sample and channels |

### 6.3 Payer warning

Under [SGB XI §34](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_11/__34.html), German long-term-care benefit claims generally suspend while the insured person is abroad, with limited provisions for temporary stays and for certain benefits in the EU, EEA and Switzerland. Georgia is outside that group. [SGB V §16](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_5/__16.html) also provides that statutory health-insurance benefit claims generally suspend while insured persons are abroad, subject to other statutory exceptions.

This does not resolve every individual case, but it is sufficient to reject any base case that assumes German public reimbursement. Austria and Switzerland also require written, case-specific analysis using official guidance, including the [Austrian Social Ministry's Pflegegeld information](https://www.sozialministerium.gv.at/Themen/Pflege/Pflegegeld.html) and the [Swiss Federal Office of Public Health guidance on benefits abroad](https://www.bag.admin.ch/de/leistungen-im-ausland-fuer-versicherte-mit-wohnsitz-im-ausland).

### 6.4 Required net-family-cost calculation

For each country and customer segment:

```text
Net family cost of Tskaltubo =
facility fee
+ therapies and services not included
+ private health/travel insurance
+ family travel and accommodation
+ guardianship, translation and administration
+ emergency transfer and repatriation provision
- benefits that remain legally payable
- verified tax or other offsets
```

Compare this with the family's realistic domestic alternative, not with a headline DACH nursing-home price.

### 6.5 Demand-validation programme

The first round should include at least the previously proposed **20–30 qualified interviews** across families, operators and referral sources, but that is only qualitative validation. Record:

- country, language and relationship to the potential resident;
- care need and dependency level;
- current arrangement and monthly out-of-pocket cost;
- benefit entitlement that would be retained or lost;
- maximum acceptable travel time and visit frequency;
- reaction to Georgia and Tskaltubo specifically;
- minimum clinical and emergency assurances;
- acceptable all-inclusive monthly or daily price;
- willingness to make a refundable reservation or proceed to assessment;
- primary objection and reason for refusal.

Before a build decision, qualitative interest must convert into observable behaviour: qualified assessments, written referral intent, reservations or deposits, subject to a lawful and transparent refund policy.

### 6.6 Rehabilitation demand engine

The current model assumes 42 rehabilitation/short-stay beds. At 65% occupancy this requires approximately **9,965 occupied bed-days per year**. Depending on a 14- or 21-day average stay, that means roughly **712 or 475 admissions per year**, or about **1.95 or 1.30 admissions per day**.

That throughput will not arise from general digital marketing. It requires a repeatable referral engine, clinical eligibility screening, travel coordination, discharge planning and clear responsibility if a patient deteriorates before or during travel.

### 6.7 Market go/no-go questions

- Is there a sufficiently large self-pay segment after benefits are removed from the calculation?
- Which segment values Georgia rather than merely tolerating it?
- Is the strongest entry product long-term care, respite, rehabilitation or a staged combination?
- Can the project reach families through trusted operators, clinicians or diaspora channels?
- Does the price remain attractive after flights, visits, insurance and repatriation provision?
- What refusal reasons can the product realistically solve?
- Can the project generate year-round rehabilitation admissions without unsafe case selection?

## 7. Regulatory, clinical and data-protection framework

### 7.1 Regulatory principle

The legal classification follows the actual service delivered, not the marketing label. Georgia's [Law on Health Care](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/29980) includes healthcare and rehabilitation concepts, and the [Law on Medical Practice](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/15334?publication=33) governs professional medical activity. The latter source itself warns when its English consolidated version is being updated, so Georgian counsel must verify the operative Georgian text and all subordinate regulations.

The project needs a service-by-service legal memorandum covering:

- long-term residential care;
- nursing services;
- medical practice and physician responsibility;
- rehabilitation and physiotherapy;
- occupational therapy or equivalent services;
- medication storage, prescribing and administration;
- laboratory, diagnostics and telemedicine interfaces;
- hydrotherapy and bathing facilities;
- medical devices and nurse-call systems;
- food safety and therapeutic diets;
- infection prevention and healthcare waste;
- ambulance and hospital transfer;
- professional qualification recognition and nursing registration;
- facility notification, permit and licence requirements.

### 7.2 Operator versus technical-services partner

A DACH company that contributes a brand, QMS, training and audits may be a **management or technical-services partner**. That does not necessarily make it the Georgian licensed operator or relieve the local operator of statutory duties.

Every external document must distinguish:

- brand licensor;
- manager or technical-services provider;
- licensed operating entity;
- licensed medical institution, if separate;
- employer of clinical staff;
- treating physician and clinical lead;
- property owner;
- party holding water, building and operating rights.

### 7.3 Required clinical-accountability matrix

| Decision or risk | Accountable role/entity to define |
|---|---|
| Admission and exclusion | Georgian medical/clinical lead and licensed OpCo |
| Care plan and acuity review | Nursing lead under approved clinical governance |
| Prescription and medication changes | Licensed prescriber under Georgian law |
| Medication procurement, storage and administration | Named licensed entity and responsible pharmacist/clinical lead as applicable |
| Rehabilitation prescription and goals | Licensed physician/rehabilitation lead |
| Hydrotherapy suitability | Medical lead using an approved protocol |
| Deterioration and emergency transfer | On-duty clinical lead and contracted ambulance/hospital network |
| Safeguarding and consent | OpCo governance with defined escalation and legal support |
| Incident disclosure to families | OpCo and named communication lead |
| Quality audit and corrective action | OpCo board plus DACH technical partner if contracted |
| Death, certification and repatriation | Georgian legal/clinical process plus family and insurer coordination |

### 7.4 Resident legal safeguards

The legal workstream must address:

- immigration and residence status for long stays;
- legal capacity, powers of attorney, guardianship and supported decision-making;
- informed consent and advance directives;
- resident contract, deposits, termination and fee increases;
- complaint handling, safeguarding and independent escalation;
- family information rights and confidentiality;
- personal property and financial abuse controls;
- death registration, funeral arrangements and international repatriation;
- continuity of care if the operator, property owner or project company fails.

### 7.5 Hydrotherapy and clinical claims

Official tourism material describes Tskaltubo as a balneological resort and reports mineral water at around 35°C. See [Georgia Travel](https://georgia.travel/tskaltubo-plaza) and the [Tskaltubo municipality](https://tskaltubo.gov.ge/en/). These are adequate sources for destination context, not for treatment efficacy.

Before any medical benefit claim, obtain:

- current chemical, microbiological and radiological water analysis;
- source, allocation, capacity and use rights;
- engineering design for delivery, storage, temperature, disinfection and drainage;
- contraindications and adverse-event protocol;
- medical review and informed consent;
- cleaning, monitoring and incident records;
- evidence review for each proposed claim;
- confirmation of facility and professional licensing requirements.

Approved interim wording:

> **Mineral-water-based hydrotherapy is being evaluated as an adjunctive service, subject to water analysis, clinical protocols, medical review, technical feasibility and applicable licensing.**

### 7.6 Emergency and hospital network

An MoU is not enough. The project needs tested service-level arrangements for:

- ambulance response and dispatch;
- 24/7 emergency-department access;
- ICU, imaging, laboratory, surgery, cardiology and stroke pathways;
- pharmacy and urgent medication supply;
- blood products and oxygen, where relevant;
- transfer documentation and language support;
- communication with families and DACH clinicians;
- travel interruption and medical repatriation.

The route and transfer time must be tested from the selected property under realistic traffic and weather conditions.

### 7.7 Data protection and cross-border health data

Health data is highly sensitive. If a DACH operator, clinician or family portal receives Georgian resident records, or EU-originating health data is transferred to Georgia, the project needs a complete controller/processor and international-transfer analysis.

Georgia is not listed among the European Commission's adequacy jurisdictions on the [current adequacy-decision page](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection/adequacy-decisions_en). Depending on the data flows, safeguards may include the Commission's [Standard Contractual Clauses](https://commission.europa.eu/publications/publications-standard-contractual-clauses-sccs_en), a transfer-impact assessment, technical and organisational measures, access controls, encryption, retention limits and incident-response obligations.

The data map must cover:

- referral records;
- pre-admission assessments;
- electronic care and medical records;
- telemedicine;
- family communications;
- payment and identity records;
- CCTV, access control and nurse-call logs;
- staff records;
- quality audits and incident reports.

## 8. Real-estate and asset audit

### 8.1 Asset-selection principle

The project should select the product and clinical programme before final asset selection. A visually impressive sanatorium is not necessarily suitable for safe care, efficient staffing or a separable pilot.

The acquisition route may be a purchase, long lease, concession, redevelopment agreement, option, management arrangement or joint venture. “Secure property rights from the Georgian government” is too narrow and assumes both ownership and willingness to transact.

### 8.3 Commercial priority versus diligence priority

The July 2026 deck appears inconsistent because it ranks Intouristi and Geologist first while recommending that Imereti, Geologist and Meshakhte be checked first. The two ideas should be separated:

- **Commercial screen:** Intouristi, Geologist, Savane, Imereti, Meshakhte.
- **Information-gap urgency:** Imereti, Geologist and Meshakhte have the largest title/status uncertainty.
- **Recommended action:** obtain fresh NAPR packages for all five in parallel because the registry-document cost is low relative to project risk.

### 8.4 Required title and transaction package

For every shortlisted property:

1. current NAPR real-estate extract;
2. cadastral plan and coordinate file;
3. ownership and transaction history;
4. mortgages, pledges, attachments and litigation;
5. servitudes and access rights;
6. leases, licences, informal possession and occupants;
7. public or auction investment obligations;
8. heritage status and restrictions;
9. utility, road and easement rights;
10. mineral-water source, allocation, connection and use rights;
11. identity and authority of the negotiating counterparty;
12. written confirmation of sale, lease, concession or JV willingness.

Use the [NAPR information-service page](https://napr.gov.ge/en/service/information-services/extract-public-register) for current procedures and fees. An extract used for an LOI should be recent and must be refreshed before signing and closing.

### 8.5 Technical red-flag scope

The first site inspection must cover:

- measured survey and reconciliation of registered, gross and net areas;
- room-bay dimensions and feasible ensuite bathrooms;
- structure and seismic performance;
- roof, façade, windows and water ingress;
- mould, asbestos, lead and other hazardous materials;
- foundations, soil, drainage and slope conditions;
- electrical capacity, generator and backup systems;
- water, wastewater, hot water and pressure;
- heating, cooling, ventilation and indoor-air quality;
- fire compartmentation, escape, sprinklers, alarms and fire-service access;
- lifts, bed evacuation and universal accessibility;
- kitchens, laundry, waste and deliveries;
- nurse call, data, security and resident wandering controls;
- hydrotherapy plant, source connection, drainage and humidity control;
- heritage constraints and façade/interior conservation;
- external works, safe walking routes and ambulance access;
- the physical boundary and independent services required for Phase 1.

### 8.6 Pilot separability test

A pilot is viable only if all of the following can be separated or allocated without creating unsafe interdependence:

- fire zone and escape routes;
- lift and bed movement;
- electrical supply and emergency power;
- water, hot water, wastewater and heating/cooling;
- kitchen, laundry, pharmacy/medication, waste and stores;
- staff access and changing;
- resident and ambulance access;
- clinical and therapy space;
- construction-site separation from occupied areas;
- licence and occupancy approval;
- operating budget and cost allocation.

If central systems must be sized and installed for the full building, a 30–50-bed pilot may not materially reduce initial capex.

### 8.7 Occupants and social safeguards

“Politically clean relocation” is unacceptable wording. Any occupied asset requires a lawful, consensual and socially responsible process led by qualified local counsel and public authorities. The project should not rely on displacement as a hidden development assumption. Occupied sites should normally be deferred until rights, consultation, compensation, housing and timing are independently resolved.

## 9. Operating model and workforce

### 9.1 Operating-model principle

The operating model must be built from resident need and 24/7 coverage, not from a top-down EBITDA target. Long-term care, rehabilitation and hospitality should have separate departmental assumptions, with shared services shown explicitly.

### 9.2 Required operating departments

| Department | Core responsibilities | Critical unresolved inputs |
|---|---|---|
| Nursing and personal care | 24/7 care, observations, ADL support, medication, documentation | Acuity, staffing ratios, qualification recognition, productive hours, night coverage |
| Medical governance | Admissions, prescribing, deterioration, review, clinical policy | Physician model, on-call coverage, liability, hospital interfaces |
| Rehabilitation | Assessment, treatment plan, physiotherapy, occupational therapy, discharge | Therapy minutes, caseload, referral criteria, equipment, outcomes |
| Hydrotherapy | Screening, supervised treatments, water safety | Protocol, contraindications, staff, plant, licensing, evidence |
| Dementia support | Cognitive/behavioural care, safeguarding, secure environment | Whether included at all in Phase 1; specialist staffing and design |
| Hospitality | Reception, rooms, housekeeping, laundry, food and activities | Service level, dietary needs, outsourcing, resident dependency |
| Sales and admissions | Enquiries, assessment, contracting, travel and onboarding | Lead sources, conversion, cancellations, travel coordination |
| Quality and safeguarding | Audit, incidents, complaints, training, corrective action | QMS ownership, board reporting, external audit rights |
| Facilities and security | MEP, life safety, grounds, access, transport | Service contracts, spares, backup utilities, construction interfaces |
| Finance and administration | Billing, payroll, procurement, cash, family accounts | Currency, deposits, bad debt, tax, segregation of resident funds |

### 9.3 Bottom-up workforce model

The model needs a roster by shift and unit. For each role, calculate:

```text
Required paid FTE =
required productive coverage hours
÷ productive hours available per paid FTE
```

Productive hours must reflect contracted hours, annual leave, public holidays, sickness, training, supervision and vacancies. The model must separately identify:

- registered nurses and legal scope of practice;
- care assistants and support workers;
- medical director and physician coverage;
- physiotherapists, occupational therapists and rehabilitation assistants;
- pharmacy/medication support;
- infection prevention and quality lead;
- social work, psychology, activities and family liaison;
- admissions, sales and travel coordination;
- kitchen, housekeeping, laundry, maintenance, transport and security;
- management, HR, finance, IT and procurement;
- agency, overtime and vacancy premiums.

### 9.4 German-language coverage

“German on every shift” is not a staffing model. Define:

- the number and competence level of German speakers on every unit and shift;
- who can conduct clinical assessment, consent, medication explanation and difficult family conversations in German;
- backup when the named speaker is absent;
- language testing at recruitment and re-testing after training;
- translation rules for medical records and emergency transfers;
- distinction between conversational A2/B1 ability and clinical B2/C1 communication.

A language academy can support development, but cannot substitute for licensed clinical competence or a robust recruitment pool.

### 9.5 Wage and recruitment evidence

Georgia's official average wage data is useful macro context, but it cannot validate the cost of German-speaking nurses or rehabilitation professionals. The [National Statistics Office of Georgia wage series](https://www.geostat.ge/en/modules/categories/39/wages) reported average monthly nominal earnings of GEL 2,363.8 in Q1 2026, but the project requires direct salary quotes for each role, language premium, social costs, shift differentials, recruitment, housing or relocation, training, overtime, agency cover and turnover.

### 9.6 Workforce diligence

- Confirm the size of the licensed nurse and therapist pool within practical commuting distance.
- Confirm recognition and registration requirements for Georgian returnees and foreign professionals.
- Test German-language supply rather than assuming diaspora availability.
- Obtain salary expectations from candidates and recruiters.
- Build safe rosters for conservative, base and high-acuity cases.
- Include leadership and educator capacity before opening.
- Define background checks, safeguarding checks and occupational health.
- Model turnover, absence, maternity leave, vacancies and training time.
- Establish staff transport and housing needs for shift work.
- Avoid any dependence on a single returning clinician or founder relationship.

### 9.7 Operating KPIs

The board and operator agreement should monitor at least:

| Domain | Measures |
|---|---|
| Demand | Qualified leads, assessments, conversion, cancellations, referral concentration |
| Occupancy | Available, occupied and out-of-service beds by product; length of stay |
| Revenue | Rate by segment, discounts, ancillary revenue, bad debt and payer concentration |
| Workforce | FTE, agency/overtime, vacancy, turnover, sick leave, training and language coverage |
| Quality | Falls, pressure injury, medication error, infection, complaint, safeguarding and readmission |
| Rehabilitation | Admissions, therapy delivery, goal attainment, interrupted stays and discharge destination |
| Finance | EBITDA, operating cash, working capital, maintenance capex and variance to budget |
| Asset | Life-safety defects, utility downtime, response time and deferred maintenance |
| Family experience | Communication compliance, complaints, response times and satisfaction |

## 10. Partner, entity and deal structure

### 10.1 Required entity map

The project needs more than “DACH partner” and “Georgian partner.” A bankable structure should identify:

| Party/entity | Primary role | Capital or responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsor / DevCo | Origination, coordination, development management and governance | Development budget, advisers, programme and sponsor accountability |
| Property owner / PropCo | Site control, building, fixed plant and landlord obligations | Equity/debt for acquisition and conversion; lifecycle capex |
| Licensed OpCo | Resident contracts, staffing, care operations and operating compliance | Working capital, licences, insurance and operating risk |
| Medical institution or clinical entity, if required | Medical and rehabilitation services | Clinical licences, physicians, medication and clinical liability |
| DACH operator or technical-services partner | Brand, QMS, training, audits, management support and referrals | Know-how, personnel, systems and contractually defined services |
| Georgian local development partner | Public authorities, construction, permits, procurement and local implementation | Local capability, relationships, execution and possibly equity |
| Acute-care and ambulance partners | Escalation, diagnostics, emergency and hospital care | SLAs, clinical acceptance and transfer capability |
| Hydrotherapy technical/clinical partner | Water system, protocols, training and technical standards | Defined specialist scope, not broad project endorsement |
| Equity investors and lenders | Long-term capital | Return requirements, covenants, draw conditions and oversight |

### 10.2 Deal structures are not mutually exclusive

The July 2026 deck presents a management contract, PropCo/OpCo and integrated JV as three alternatives. They are different analytical dimensions and can coexist.

For example, a local-investor PropCo may lease to a Georgian licensed OpCo, while a DACH company provides brand and management services and owns a minority share of the OpCo.

### 10.3 Structure comparison

| Structure | DACH capital | DACH control | Project benefit | Principal risk |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Technical-services or management agreement | Low | Contractual only | Fastest asset-light entry | Brand and quality exposure without full operational control |
| Brand licence plus audit | Very low | Limited | Narrow scope | Can become superficial unless audit and termination rights are strong |
| PropCo/OpCo with DACH OpCo participation | Moderate | Operational and governance rights | Clearer allocation of real-estate and operating risks | Rent, capex and covenant misalignment |
| Integrated equity JV | High | High if governance is balanced | Full upside and unified control | Highest capital, construction and country exposure |
| Pilot operating agreement in leased/allocated wing | Low to moderate | Defined pilot scope | Tests demand and operations | Central infrastructure and separation may defeat the cost advantage |

### 10.4 Asset-light is not the same as economically viable

An asset-light structure may make the opportunity attractive to a DACH operator by limiting its capital. It does not improve the underlying unlevered project yield. The PropCo still needs an acceptable return and the OpCo must afford rent after staffing and quality costs.

The integrated model therefore needs:

- sustainable rent or availability-payment capacity;
- fixed and variable rent scenarios;
- rent cover and lender debt-service coverage;
- responsibility for initial, lifecycle and replacement capex;
- security deposits, guarantees and cure rights;
- operator step-in and replacement provisions;
- resident continuity if either entity defaults.

### 10.5 Minimum management/technical-services agreement topics

- exact services and deliverables;
- pre-opening plan and staffing support;
- QMS source documents and localisation rights;
- clinical responsibility and excluded decisions;
- brand standards and approval rights;
- audit access, frequency, reporting and remediation;
- management fee, performance fee and reimbursable cost rules;
- referral obligations, if any, and prohibition on unsubstantiated volume promises;
- data protection and international data transfers;
- insurance, indemnity and liability caps;
- reserved matters and budget approval;
- key-person dependency and replacement;
- termination, de-branding and transition;
- resident-safety and continuity plan;
- anti-bribery, sanctions and public-authority engagement rules.

### 10.6 Correct role for letters of intent

A broad LOI should not be used to manufacture credibility before scope is defined. The sequence from the 2024 brief, a spa LOI followed by a ministry LOI followed by investor outreach, is not bankable.

A useful conditional expression of interest should state:

- the precise party and proposed role;
- what information has and has not been reviewed;
- the pilot or service scope under discussion;
- that it is non-binding except for stated provisions;
- conditions precedent, including title, licences, design, capex, financing and governance;
- no promise of referrals, occupancy, clinical outcomes or capital unless explicitly agreed;
- confidentiality, publicity and logo-use rules;
- expiry date and next diligence steps.

### 11.6 Capex concerns

- EUR 1,050/m² has no asset-specific measured scope or market tender support.
- Adaptive reuse can carry demolition, temporary works, hazardous materials, heritage and unknown-condition costs not visible in a generic rate.
- Fire, seismic, lifts, ensuite bathrooms, medical gases if required, backup power and hydrotherapy humidity control can be major cost drivers.
- The 15% contingency may be insufficient before intrusive survey and concept design.
- Pre-opening and working capital of EUR 1.0m is not tied to recruitment lead times, training, marketing, initial stock, deposits or ramp losses.
- VAT, customs, import, professional fees, permitting, insurance, financing fees, interest during construction and currency exposure are not explicitly modelled.
- Site infrastructure, roads, landscaping, utility reinforcement, water connection and wastewater upgrades need separate lines.
- Replacement capex and lifecycle renewal require a long-term schedule, not only a flat 2% maintenance assumption.

### 11.7 Operating assumptions for 140 beds

The full-scale model allocates 98 beds to long-term care and 42 to rehabilitation/short stay.

| Assumption | Conservative | Base | Upside |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Long-term monthly rate | EUR 2,600 | EUR 3,400 | EUR 4,200 |
| Long-term occupancy | 75% | 85% | 90% |
| Rehabilitation daily rate | EUR 145 | EUR 190 | EUR 240 |
| Rehabilitation occupancy | 50% | 65% | 75% |
| Ancillary revenue | 3% of core revenue | 6% of core revenue | 8% of core revenue |
| EBITDA margin | 14% | 23% | 28% |
| Maintenance capex | 2% of revenue | 2% of revenue | 2% of revenue |

The prices, occupancies and EBITDA margins are assumptions. The margin is not yet derived from staffing and departmental costs.

### 11.8 Revenue formulas

```text
Long-term revenue =
98 beds × monthly rate × 12 months × long-term occupancy

Rehabilitation revenue =
42 beds × daily rate × 365 days × rehabilitation occupancy

Core revenue =
long-term revenue + rehabilitation revenue

Total revenue =
core revenue × (1 + ancillary-revenue percentage)

EBITDA =
total revenue × assumed EBITDA margin

Operating cash flow before financing and tax =
EBITDA - (2% × total revenue)

Unlevered pre-tax cash yield =
operating cash flow before financing and tax ÷ total investment
```

```text
Long-term revenue
= 98 × EUR 3,400 × 12 × 85%
= EUR 3,398,640

= 42 × EUR 190 × 365 × 65%
```

The next model must include:

- monthly design, construction, commissioning and occupancy ramp;
- service-line revenue by long-term care, rehabilitation, hydrotherapy and any other module;
- discounts, cancellations, bad debt, deposits and refunds;
- resident dependency/acuity mix and care hours;
- bottom-up staffing roster and wage build;
- food, housekeeping, laundry, medical supplies, therapy consumables and pharmacy;
- utilities, maintenance, security, transport, insurance and professional fees;
- brand, management, referral and technical-service fees;
- Georgian tax, VAT, customs and withholding;
- currency exposure by GEL, EUR, USD and CHF where relevant;
- debt draw, interest during construction, fees, amortisation, covenants and DSCR;
- working capital and ramp-up losses;
- lifecycle and replacement capex;
- separate PropCo and OpCo income statements, cash flows and balance-sheet assumptions;
- rent affordability and landlord return;
- exit value, terminal assumptions, NPV, project IRR and equity IRR;
- downside cases including delay, lower rate, lower occupancy, higher staff cost, higher capex and operator withdrawal;
- sponsor development cost and equity at risk;
- closure, de-branding and resident-continuity reserve.

### 11.17 Financial go/no-go tests

No site should proceed to binding control unless:

- an independent cost consultant has produced an asset-specific range with exclusions and risk allowances;
- the pilot has standalone capex and operating economics;
- the base case works on private payment without public reimbursement;
- staffing costs are based on market evidence and safe rosters;
- the model funds ramp-up and downside liquidity;
- rent and debt are supportable under a downside case;
- the investor return remains viable without an unsupported exit multiple;
- the sponsor can identify actual equity and contingency funding.

- sponsor, PropCo and OpCo term-sheet structure;

**Required outputs:**

- rehabilitation referral conversion and outcomes;

- complaint, incident and family experience;
- construction performance and updated expansion capex.

## 14. Commercial validation and outreach

### 14.1 Correct immediate outreach objective

The immediate operator conversation is a discovery call, not a financing pitch. The question is whether the operator's competence, risk appetite and operating model fit a controlled pilot in Georgia.

### 14.2 Operator qualification criteria

The “2–3+ sites” criterion in the current deck is only a proxy. A stronger screen is:

- demonstrated multi-site operating systems;
- relevant mix of long-term care, rehabilitation and dementia experience;
- audited quality outcomes and incident governance;
- ability to localise SOPs and train a new workforce;
- cross-border or management-contract experience;
- clear brand-risk and liability boundaries;
- access to families, clinicians or referral channels;
- willingness to support a pilot without promising unverified occupancy;
- financial capacity to fund its agreed role;
- management attention and named senior sponsor.

### 14.3 Twenty-minute discovery call

Use the call to test:

1. Which service line is credible as Phase 1?
2. What resident acuity would the operator accept?
3. Would it act as operator, manager, technical adviser, brand licensor or investor?
4. What minimum local clinical and legal structure would it require?
5. What QMS, staffing and audit requirements are non-negotiable?
6. Can it credibly support referrals, or only brand and know-how?
7. What capex, scale and pilot format would make the opportunity worth further diligence?
8. What would make it decline immediately?

### 14.4 Approved short pitch

> We are evaluating the phased adaptive reuse of a historic sanatorium in Tskaltubo, Georgia, for a German-speaking long-term-care residence with a separately governed rehabilitation and mineral-water hydrotherapy component. The concept is at pre-feasibility stage. We are looking for an experienced operator or technical-services partner to test the Phase 1 service model, quality system, clinical boundaries and asset-light participation structure. The base case is private-pay, and any pilot would proceed only after property, licensing, demand, staffing and capex diligence.

### 14.5 Communication rules

- State what is known, assumed and unknown.
- Use current dates on property and transport claims.
- Never imply ministry, municipality or agency endorsement from a screenshot or logo.
- Do not call a technical partner the operator unless it accepts that legal role.
- Do not describe non-binding interest as a commitment.
- Do not use clinical benefit claims without a claims file and medical/legal approval.
- Do not publish returns without the metric, stage, assumptions and exclusions.
- Do not lead with a specific asset until current title and access are verified.

### Appendix E: Metric definitions

| Metric | Definition | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Occupancy | Occupied bed-days divided by available bed-days | Must exclude beds genuinely out of service and be reported by product |
| Average daily/monthly rate | Revenue for the relevant stay type divided by occupied days/months | Discounts and included services must be consistent |
| EBITDA | Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation | Not cash flow, profit after tax or distributable cash |
| Maintenance capex | Recurring capital needed to maintain operations | A flat percentage may understate lifecycle replacement |
| Stabilised unlevered pre-tax cash flow | EBITDA less maintenance capex before debt and tax in a mature operating year | Ignores ramp-up, construction timing and financing |
| Cash yield | Stabilised unlevered pre-tax cash flow divided by total investment | Not IRR; does not reflect timing or terminal value |
| Project IRR | Discount rate at which total unlevered project cash flows have zero NPV | Highly sensitive to exit value and timing |
| Equity IRR | Investor return after debt flows and equity contributions/distributions | Can be increased by leverage while risk also rises |
| DSCR | Cash available for debt service divided by debt service | Requires a lender-agreed definition |
| Rent cover | OpCo earnings or cash available for rent divided by rent | Must be tested under downside staffing and occupancy |
